01 HER STORY

A quiet revolution

She made
the unseen
matter.

Marie Curie turned curiosity into a force of nature. Born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw, she crossed borders, disciplines, and expectations to investigate the strange rays released by certain minerals.

With Pierre Curie, she began a patient investigation into pitchblende, a mineral that seemed more active than its known ingredients could explain. Their work revealed two new elements: polonium, named for her homeland, and radium, a word that would become synonymous with invisible energy.

Her achievements were scientific, but also deeply human. She worked in difficult conditions, carried knowledge across national boundaries, and insisted that her discoveries belong to everyone. During the First World War, she helped bring mobile X-ray units to the front, giving doctors a clearer view inside wounded bodies.

Curie remains the only person to receive Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields. Her life is a reminder that progress often begins with a question no one else thinks is worth asking, followed by the courage to stay with it.

02 THE JOURNEY

A life in discoveries

Moments that
shifted the future.

  1. 1891

    A new beginning

    Maria moves to Paris to study physics and mathematics at the Sorbonne, graduating at the top of her class.

  2. 1898

    New elements

    Marie and Pierre Curie announce the discovery of polonium and radium after years of painstaking work.

  3. 1903

    First Nobel Prize

    She shares the Nobel Prize in Physics, becoming the first woman to receive the honor.

  4. 1911

    A second field

    Her Nobel Prize in Chemistry recognizes the isolation of radium and her study of its compounds.

  5. 1914

    Science in service

    She develops mobile radiography units, bringing X-ray technology closer to wounded soldiers.
